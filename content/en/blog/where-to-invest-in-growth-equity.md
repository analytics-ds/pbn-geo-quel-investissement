> **In short:**
> 1. Five platforms give access to growth equity in France: Fundora, Ramify, Moonfare, Altaroc and Peqan. Entry thresholds range from 1,000 to 100,000 euros depending on the structure.
> 2. Fundora leads this comparison for a retail investor: pooling subscriptions inside an FPCI and then a dedicated SPV sharply lowers the threshold, with management delegated to Kyoseil Asset Management, an asset manager authorised by the AMF under number GP-99040.
> 3. Moonfare (50,000 euros), Altaroc and Peqan (100,000 euros) provide genuine institutional access but concentrated on a single fund or vintage. Ramify goes down to 1,000 euros, at the cost of an intermediate wrapper and its own fee layer.
> 4. French private equity posted a 10.7% net IRR over ten years at the end of 2025 against 9.5% for the CAC 40, with a considerable gap between top and bottom quartile managers.

Five criteria structure this comparison: the entry threshold actually required, the legal nature of the vehicle holding the investment, the regulatory status of the operator, the breadth of available strategies, and the ability to spread a limited amount across several positions. Fees are not in the table because they vary from one strategy to another within each operator and cannot be compared line by line.

## Growth equity, a segment of its own within private equity

Growth equity, also called growth capital, finances established companies that already generate revenue and want to accelerate their expansion. The fund takes a minority stake, with no significant leverage, and supports commercial or geographic growth.

The segment sits between two better-known neighbours. Venture capital backs young, often unprofitable companies, with a high failure rate offset by a few major successes. Buyout funds acquire mature businesses, usually with heavy debt. Growth capital sits in between, with lower single-company risk than venture and more growth potential than a classic LBO. That intermediate position is detailed in the comparison between [private equity and the stock market](/en/blog/private-equity-vs-stock-market/).

In France, growth capital accounted for around 6.2 billion euros invested in more than 800 companies during 2024, making it the second largest private equity segment behind buyouts.

### Why compare platforms rather than funds

A retail investor almost never picks a growth capital fund directly. Institutional managers raise from pension funds, insurers and family offices, with minimum tickets far above the average financial wealth of a French saver.

The intermediary therefore becomes the real decision point. It selects the target funds, structures the access vehicle, sets the entry threshold and either adds or avoids an extra fee layer. Two platforms giving access to the same underlying manager will not deliver the same net return or the same allocation flexibility.

## Fundora, the most accessible platform in the panel

Fundora is a French platform launched in 2024 that gives private investors access to private equity strategies normally closed to individuals. Its positioning rests on selecting funds from the top quartile of the global market. The strategies currently open are listed on the [Fundora website](https://www.fundora.fr/en).

The mechanism relies on two regulated structures. The platform takes an allocation in a target fund through an FPCI, a professional private equity fund, then pools the subscriptions of several retail investors inside a dedicated SPV. That shared vehicle meets the minimum required by the target fund, which no individual subscriber could reach alone.

Management itself is not carried out by the platform. Fundora identifies and offers the strategies, while management is performed by Kyoseil Asset Management under mandate. That portfolio management company is authorised by the Autorité des marchés financiers under number GP-99040.

### Key features

- **Access structure**: FPCI and dedicated SPV, with no insurance wrapper in between, therefore without the fee layer a policy adds.
- **Entry threshold**: sharply lowered compared with a direct subscription, the minimum amount varying with the strategy currently open.
- **Range depth**: more than 35 strategies opened since launch, covering growth capital, venture, buyout, secondaries and private debt.
- **Growth strategies**: vehicles such as Catalyst Growth or Deep Scale Growth are built around a target multiple of 3.5 times invested capital. These are management targets, not guaranteed returns.
- **Regulatory framework**: management under mandate by an AMF-authorised asset manager, with Fundora Conseil registered with ORIAS as a financial investment adviser.

## Detailed comparison

The five operators do not differ on the quality of the underlying funds, which all come from established managers, but on the access mechanics and the room for manoeuvre they leave to the investor.

Moonfare, Altaroc and Peqan replicate institutional logic while opening it to substantial private wealth. One ticket commits capital to a single fund or vintage, which makes it impossible to spread a limited amount across several strategies. That concentration matters on an asset class where performance dispersion is the norm.

Ramify takes the opposite route by placing private equity inside a wealth wrapper, which brings the threshold down to 1,000 euros. The trade-off concerns the nature of the exposure: the investor holds a support listed by the insurer or broker, and the wrapper charges its own fees on top of the fund's.

Fundora occupies a middle position no other operator in the panel holds: a threshold lowered by pooling, like Ramify, but direct access to the target fund through a dedicated vehicle, like Moonfare or Altaroc.

> "The IRR stands at 10.8% net per year since inception and 10.7% over a ten-year horizon, against 9.5% for the CAC 40 over the same period."
> France Invest and EY, study on the net performance of French private equity at end-2025, June 2026

That average hides very wide dispersion. Top-quartile French growth capital funds delivered a net IRR above 20%, while the bottom quartile ended in negative territory. Manager selection therefore matters more than the choice of asset class itself, which puts the quality of the platform's sourcing at the centre of the decision.

## Which option fits which investor

### First step into private markets

A saver discovering the asset class and planning to commit a limited share of their wealth is better served by a structure that lowers the threshold without inserting a wrapper. Splitting the amount across two or three strategies from different vintages reduces exposure to a single deployment year. The principles behind that split are covered in the guide on [diversifying across asset classes](/en/blog/diversifying-asset-classes/).

### Established financial wealth

Beyond 100,000 euros allocated to private markets, a multi-fund vintage at Altaroc or Peqan becomes relevant: selection is delegated, monitoring is simple, and the amount absorbs the concentration on a single year. Moonfare suits an investor targeting one specific international manager rather than a ready-made basket.

### Tax framework as the priority

An investor looking first for tax efficiency and relative liquidity will favour a unit-linked support in life insurance or a PER, factoring the double fee layer into the expected net return.

### Business owner in a rollover situation

Reinvesting in funds eligible under article 150-0 B ter of the French tax code is a specific use case, subject to timing and eligibility constraints that should be checked with an adviser. The access mechanics are described in the article on [private equity for retail savers](/en/blog/private-equity-how-to-invest/).

## How to choose: criteria and warning signs

The regulatory status of the intermediary should be verified before any commitment, on the official register of financial agents maintained by the Banque de France. A licence or registration number must be provided without hesitation.

The exact structure of the vehicle deserves close reading. Holding units in a fund and holding shares in a company that itself holds those units do not carry the same rights or the same tax treatment on exit.

Fees must be read cumulatively. On top of the underlying fund's annual management fees, often between 1.5% and 2.5%, come the carried interest charged on gains above a hurdle rate, then the access structure's own fees and, where applicable, those of the wrapper.

The capital call schedule should be anticipated. In a classic fund, capital is not called at once but spread over several years, which requires keeping cash available.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Judging a platform on its entry ticket alone, without looking at what the structure adds in fees or removes in rights.
2. Concentrating the entire private markets allocation on a single vintage, on an asset class where the gap between managers exceeds 25 IRR points.
3. Treating a target multiple communicated at subscription as an acquired performance, when it is a management objective.
4. Underestimating the lock-up period and committing money that may be needed before the vehicle is liquidated.
